What "casa" means

In Philippine car talk, the casa is the brand's authorized dealership, as opposed to a second-hand lot or an independent shop. When people say "sa casa ko binili" or "sa casa ko pina-PMS," they mean an authorized dealer of the brand. A casa sells brand-new units on official SRPs, arranges bank financing, and handles service and warranty work under the brand's standards.

Documents to prepare

  • Everyone: two valid government IDs with matching signatures, and proof of billing
  • Employed: certificate of employment with compensation, recent payslips or latest ITR
  • Business owner / self-employed: DTI or SEC registration, business or mayor's permit, latest ITR or financial statements, bank statements
  • OFW / seafarer: employment or crew contract, payslips or proof of remittance, and SPA or co-borrower details for the family member in the Philippines

PMS at an authorized casa

PMS (preventive maintenance service) follows the schedule in your owner's manual, by kilometers or months, whichever comes first. Many owners keep PMS at an authorized casa because the work follows the brand's schedule, uses genuine parts and fluids, and leaves a service record that supports warranty claims and resale value. Ask about the free PMS labor included in the all-in package. Read the PMS schedule guide.

Will the monthly fit?

A sensible guide: keep the monthly amortization at about 30–35% of your gross monthly income. Divide the monthly by 0.35 for a minimum income and by 0.30 for a comfortable one. A spouse or family member can sometimes join as a co-borrower so the bank considers combined income. The bank always makes the final call.
