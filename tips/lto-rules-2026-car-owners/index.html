LTO Rules 2026: Checklist for Car Owners

By Richard Cuagdan, Mitsubishi Sales Executive · 2026-10-21

Quick answer: In 2026, brand-new dealer-registered vehicles get a 5-year initial registration (from February 15), fake or improvised plates can get your car impounded (since September 1), apprehended drivers have 15 working days to settle fines instead of on-the-spot license confiscation, and the e-license in LTMS/eGovPH is valid.

Ang daming bagong LTO rules ngayong 2026. Here's a quick checklist so you're updated, whether you just got a brand-new car or you've been driving for years. Rules can still be updated, so always check the LTO's official announcements.

1. 5-year initial registration for brand-new cars

Starting February 15, 2026, brand-new cars and motorcycles registered through dealers get a five-year initial registration, up from three years. What's still required:

  • Compulsory third-party liability (CTPL) insurance must stay valid.
  • A roadworthiness check is required around the fourth year.
  • After the five years, registration is renewed yearly.
  • Used vehicles don't qualify. Check the expiry printed on your Certificate of Registration (CR).

2. Fake and improvised plates: impound risk

Since September 1, 2026, the LTO has been cracking down on fake and improvised plates, and violators risk having their vehicles impounded. If your plates aren't out yet, ask your dealer about the status and keep your official registration documents in the car. Don't make your own "commemorative" or printed plates.

3. 15 working days to settle fines

Under the LTO's new enforcement rules, there's no more on-the-spot confiscation of licenses. Apprehended drivers are given 15 working days to settle their fines. Settle on time to avoid further penalties.

4. Your e-license is valid

The electronic driver's license you can access through the LTMS portal or the eGovPH app is recognized as valid. Make sure your account is set up and you can open it quickly when asked.

5. Pedestrian safety: license revocation

An October 9, 2026 LTO memorandum states that drivers who endanger pedestrians may have their licenses revoked. Slow down near crossings and schools, and always yield at pedestrian lanes.

Quick owner checklist

  • Check the registration expiry on your CR
  • Keep CTPL valid
  • Use only official plates
  • Set up your LTMS / eGovPH account
  • Settle any violation within 15 working days
  • Yield to pedestrians, always

Frequently asked questions

Is LTO registration now 5 years for new cars?

Yes. Since February 15, 2026, brand-new cars and motorcycles registered through dealers get a five-year initial registration instead of three. After that, renewal is yearly, with a roadworthiness check required around the fourth year. Used vehicles don't qualify.

Can my car be impounded for improvised plates?

Yes. The LTO crackdown on fake or improvised plates, in effect since September 1, 2026, can lead to impoundment. Use the plates and documents issued for your vehicle.

Will the LTO confiscate my license on the spot?

Under the new enforcement rules, there's no on-the-spot license confiscation; apprehended drivers are given 15 working days to settle their fines.

Is the digital driver's license valid?

Yes. The e-license accessed through LTMS or the eGovPH app is recognized as valid.

Can the LTO revoke a license for endangering pedestrians?

An October 9, 2026 LTO memorandum says drivers who endanger pedestrians may lose their licenses.
