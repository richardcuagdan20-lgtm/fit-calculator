OFW Car-Buying Guide: Buying a Car for Your Family Back Home

By Richard Cuagdan, Car Sales Executive · 2026-10-21

Buying a car while working abroad is very doable. Many of my clients are OFWs who handle everything online while their family in the Philippines does the viewing, signing or pickup.

Decide who the borrower is

There are usually two setups. You can apply as the principal borrower with a family member as co-borrower, or a family member in the Philippines applies with your remittances supporting the application. Banks have different policies, so I'll help you choose the setup most likely to be approved for your case.

Prepare a Special Power of Attorney

If you can't be here to sign, an SPA lets a trusted person sign for you. An SPA executed abroad generally needs to be authenticated (apostilled, or consularized where applicable) before banks will accept it. Ask me for the exact wording the bank requires before you have it notarized, so you only do it once.

Documents to gather

  • Passport and work visa or permit
  • Employment contract or certificate of employment
  • Recent payslips or remittance history
  • IDs and proof of billing of your co-borrower or attorney-in-fact

The full checklist is in the car loan requirements guide.

Choosing the right car for the family

Think about who will drive it most and where. For school runs and family trips, a 7-seater like the Xpander is popular. If the car will also earn income, the L300 or Triton may fit better. For a first car in the city, the Mirage G4 keeps monthly costs lower.

Paying while abroad

Monthly amortizations are usually paid through post-dated checks or auto-debit from a Philippine bank account. Make sure the account is funded on time each month, especially around holidays when remittances can take longer.

Working across time zones

Message me on Messenger whenever it's convenient for you. I'll coordinate with your family for viewing, test drive and release.
