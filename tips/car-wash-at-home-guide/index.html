Car Wash at Home: Step-by-Step Detailing Guide

By Richard Cuagdan, Mitsubishi Sales Executive · 2026-10-22

Quick answer: For a proper car wash at home: Wash your car in the shade with car shampoo (not dish soap), use the two-bucket method and a microfiber mitt, rinse well and dry with a microfiber towel. Clean the interior regularly, protect paint from bird droppings and sap quickly, and consider professional detailing for deep cleaning or paint protection.

A clean car isn't only about pogi points. Regular washing protects your paint, keeps the cabin healthy, and helps resale value. Here's how to do it right at home.

What you need

  • Two buckets: one with car shampoo, one with clean rinse water
  • Microfiber wash mitt and drying towels (a microfiber cloth comes with new-car freebies)
  • Separate brush or mitt for wheels
  • Glass cleaner and interior cleaner
  • Vacuum

Exterior wash, step by step

  1. Park in the shade

    Let the paint cool before washing.

  2. Rinse first

    Remove loose dirt and sand that could scratch.

  3. Wheels first

    Use a separate brush so brake dust doesn't touch the paint.

  4. Two-bucket wash, top to bottom

    Dip the mitt in shampoo, wash a panel, rinse the mitt in clean water, repeat.

  5. Rinse and dry

    Dry with a clean microfiber towel to avoid water spots.

Interior cleaning

  • Remove mats, vacuum seats and floors
  • Wipe dashboard and touchpoints: steering wheel, shifter, handles
  • Clean glass inside to reduce night glare
  • Dry wet mats fully to avoid odor

Protect the paint

  • Remove bird droppings and tree sap quickly; they can etch paint in the heat
  • Wax or sealant adds a layer of protection
  • Use a sunshade and park in the shade when possible
  • After a flood or muddy road, rinse the underside too

When to go professional

For deep interior shampooing, swirl removal or ceramic coating, a professional detailer has the right tools. Ask exactly what's included and how long protection lasts. A well-kept car pays you back later; see my resale value tips.

Frequently asked questions

Can I wash my car with dish soap?

It's better to use car shampoo. Dish soap can strip protective wax and dry out rubber and trim.

How often should I wash my car?

Every week or two for most owners, and sooner after rain, mud or bird droppings.

Why wash in the shade?

Sun dries soap and water quickly, leaving spots and streaks.

Is professional detailing worth it?

For deep interior cleaning, paint correction or ceramic coating, a professional detailer has the tools and products. Ask what's included and how long it lasts.
