Child Car Seat Law Philippines (RA 11229): Parent Guide

By Richard Cuagdan, Mitsubishi Sales Executive · 2026-10-22

Quick answer: The child car seat law in the Philippines is RA 11229. Under RA 11229, the Child Safety in Motor Vehicles Act, children 12 and below must ride in an appropriate, approved child restraint system unless they are at least 150 cm (59 inches) tall and can use the regular seat belt. Children 12 and below can't sit in the front seat unless they meet that height and are belted, and a child must never be left alone in a vehicle.

Sa Pilipinas, kandong is love, pero sa sasakyan, it's risky. In a sudden stop, even a careful parent can't hold a child in place. That's why the Child Safety in Motor Vehicles Act (RA 11229) exists. Here's what it means for your family car.

The rule in one sentence

Children 12 years old and below must be secured in an appropriate child restraint system (car seat) whenever the engine is running or the car is on the road, unless they are at least 150 cm (59 inches) tall and properly secured with the regular seat belt.

Back seat for 12 and below

No child 12 and below may sit in the front seat unless they meet the 150 cm height requirement and are properly belted. The LTO also notes that car seats should be mounted in a rear seat.

  • Shorter than 150 cm: car seat, in the back
  • 150 cm or taller: regular seat belt; front seat allowed if properly belted
  • Never leave a child alone in the car, even if they're buckled in

Choosing the right seat

The law requires a seat that fits your child's age, height and weight and that complies with the DTI standard and recognized UN regulations. In practice, seats come in stages:

  1. Rear-facing infant seat

    For babies. Rear-facing protects the head and neck best.

  2. Forward-facing seat with harness

    For toddlers who have outgrown the rear-facing limits of their seat.

  3. Booster seat

    For older kids, so the adult seat belt crosses the shoulder and hips correctly.

  4. Regular seat belt

    Once your child is at least 150 cm tall.

Follow the height and weight limits printed on the seat itself, and keep the manual.

Installation checklist

  • Read both the car seat manual and your car's owner's manual
  • Use the car's ISOFIX/anchor points if your seat and car support them, or the seat belt routing in the seat manual
  • The seat shouldn't move more than a little side to side at the belt path
  • Harness straps snug, chest clip at armpit level
  • No thick jackets under the harness
  • Ask me to show you the anchor points on release day

Frequently asked questions

Who needs a car seat in the Philippines?

Children 12 years old and below who are shorter than 150 cm (59 inches). Once a child reaches 150 cm, they can use the regular seat belt.

Can my child sit in the front seat?

Not if they're 12 or below, unless they're at least 150 cm tall and properly secured with the regular seat belt.

What kind of car seat is allowed?

One appropriate to your child's age, height and weight, and compliant with the standards set by the DTI, including recognized UN regulations. Look for the approval markings on the seat.

Are there exceptions?

The law lists cases where a car seat would put the child in greater danger, such as medical emergencies or a child's medical or developmental condition, plus others in the implementing rules.
