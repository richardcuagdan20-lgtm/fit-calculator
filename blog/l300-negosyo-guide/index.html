Mitsubishi L300 Negosyo Guide for Small Business

By Richard Cuagdan, Mitsubishi Sales Executive · 2026-10-21

Quick answer: The Mitsubishi L300 is a 2.2L turbo-diesel workhorse sold as a cab and chassis (for a custom body) or as a ready-to-work Dual AC unit. It suits deliveries, hauling, catering and retail. Pick the body for your cargo, never overload past the rated capacity, and compare monthly costs against what you now pay for deliveries.

Pag lumalaki na ang orders, the delivery fees and the "sino maghahatid?" stress add up. For many Filipino small businesses, the next step is owning a truck. The L300 has long been a familiar sight on Philippine roads because it's built for exactly that job.

L300 at a glance

  • Engine: 2.2L 16-valve DOHC (4N14) turbo-intercooled diesel, Euro 4
  • Transmission: 5-speed manual
  • Variants: Cab & Chassis 2.2D M/T (official SRP ₱829,000, MMPC price list as of August 2026) and the 2026 L300 Dual AC

Cab and chassis vs ready-to-work body

The cab and chassis gives you the truck without a rear body, so you can have one built for your business: a dropside for hardware and construction supplies, an aluminum van for goods that need protection, or a specialized body for food or catering. The Dual AC unit is ready to work from release day with air-conditioning front and rear. Ask me which bodies are available and how the build affects the timeline.

Load planning in the real world

  • Know your typical load: count your heaviest regular order, not your average.
  • Stay within the rated capacity: overloading wears the suspension, brakes and tires faster and is a safety risk. Check the official specs for your exact variant and body.
  • Secure the cargo: use straps, nets or racks so the load doesn't shift.
  • Plan routes: know your delivery areas' truck bans and road restrictions.

Business financing and documents

Business owners finance trucks all the time. Banks commonly ask for:

  • DTI or SEC registration, and mayor's/business permit
  • Latest ITR and financial statements
  • Business bank statements for the past several months
  • Valid IDs and proof of billing

More detail in my car loan requirements guide and car loan myths post. Approval is subject to bank evaluation.

Maintenance for heavy-use units

  • Follow the PMS schedule in the owner's manual. Heavy daily use may call for the severe-service intervals.
  • Check tires, brakes and suspension often if you carry heavy loads.
  • Use the correct diesel and keep the fuel filter serviced.
  • Keep a simple log of mileage and service so nothing is missed.

To keep fuel costs down, see my fuel-saving tips.

ROI thinking: delivery fees vs owning your truck

Do this simple exercise before buying:

  1. Add up what you pay now

    Monthly delivery fees, lalamove or courier costs, and lost orders when no one could deliver.

  2. Estimate ownership costs

    Your monthly amortization plus fuel, insurance, PMS, a driver's pay (if any), tolls and parking.

  3. Count the upside

    More orders you can accept, faster deliveries, and new routes or services you could offer.

  4. Compare

    If owning costs about the same or less and lets you grow, the truck can pay for itself over time.

Frequently asked questions

What L300 variants are available in 2026?

What engine does the L300 have?
