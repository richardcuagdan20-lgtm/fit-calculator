1. The guide in one page

  • Down payment: a bigger DP means a smaller loan and lower monthly, making the income ratio easier to meet. Options depend on the bank and current dealer offer.
  • The 30% rule: many PH banks cap the monthly amortization at about 30% of gross monthly income (RCBC and UCPB Savings publish 30%). Plan for 30–35% at most, including other loans.
  • Credit standing: adverse records (past-due cards, unpaid loans) can lead to denial. Get your own CIC credit report via CIC's accredited partners before applying.
  • Stability: banks look at employment tenure (often 1–2 years) or profitable business years (often 2+).
  • Documents: complete, consistent and verifiable. Same name, address and employer details everywhere.
  • Raise your chances: right-size the unit/DP, add a co-borrower, show all income with proof, clean your credit, answer verification calls.
  • Common denial reasons: monthly too high for income, adverse credit, short tenure, incomplete/inconsistent docs, unverifiable income.

2. Document checklist per employment type

Typical lists based on published BDO, Metrobank and RCBC requirements. Each bank may ask for more or less.

Locally employed

  • Application form
  • Valid government ID(s)
  • Certificate of Employment with compensation
  • Latest payslips
  • ITR / BIR Form 2316
  • Proof of billing

Self-employed / business

  • Application form
  • Valid government ID(s)
  • DTI/SEC registration, business permit
  • Latest ITR
  • Financial statements
  • Bank statements (recent months)
  • Proof of billing

OFW / seafarer

  • Application form
  • Passport / valid ID
  • Contract or COE with income
  • Proof of remittance
  • SPA / representative in PH (if required)
  • Proof of billing

Reading it: if G ≤ F, option 1 fits your income. If only H ≤ F, the bigger DP is the safer choice. If neither, consider a lower variant, a co-borrower or a bigger DP. Also budget for fuel, insurance renewal, PMS and parking.

4. Bank comparison tips

  • Compare the total amount payable over the term, not just the monthly.
  • Ask for the add-on or effective rate, term options and any fees.
  • Check the bank's minimum income, tenure and age rules before applying.
  • Ask what's in the all-in package (registration, insurance, chattel mortgage).
  • Ask about penalties for late payment and for paying off early.
  • Bank vs in-house: banks are usually cheaper but stricter; in-house may be more flexible but may cost more.
  • Let your sales executive submit to more than one partner bank.

5. Step-by-step timeline

  2. Choose – unit, variant, down payment and term.
  3. Prepare – complete the checklist for your employment type.
  4. Submit – your sales executive submits to partner banks.
  5. Verify – the bank checks credit, calls you/your employer, may visit your address.
  6. Decision – the bank issues approval and terms (timing varies per bank and on how complete your file is).
  7. Release – sign, settle initial costs if any, drive home.
