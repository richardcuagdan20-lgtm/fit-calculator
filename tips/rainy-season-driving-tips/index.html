Rainy Season Driving Tips: See, Slow, Space

By Richard Cuagdan, Mitsubishi Sales Executive · 2026-10-22

Quick answer: Rainy season driving tips, in short. In heavy rain, slow down, double your following distance, turn on your headlights (not hazards while moving), and avoid sudden steering or braking. If the car hydroplanes, ease off the gas and steer straight until the tires grip again. Before the rainy months, check wipers, tire tread and all lights.

Biglang buhos ng ulan sa Quirino Highway o sa NLEX? Rain changes everything: you see less, you're seen less, and your tires grip less. Remember three words: see, slow, space.

See and be seen

  • Turn on your headlights when it rains, even in the daytime
  • Don't drive with hazards on while moving; your turn signals won't be visible
  • Use the AC and defogger to clear fogged glass quickly
  • Keep the windshield clean inside and out; a dirty inside glass glares at night

Slow down and stay smooth

  • Lower your speed: wet roads need longer stopping distances
  • Avoid sudden braking, accelerating and steering
  • The first minutes of rain are often the slipperiest, as oil and dust lift off the road
  • Watch out for painted lane markings and steel plates, which get slippery

Give yourself space

Double your following distance. If you keep 3 seconds behind the car ahead in dry weather, make it 6 or more in heavy rain. Stay out of the spray of trucks and buses where you can't see.

If the car hydroplanes

  1. Don't panic or brake hard

    Sudden braking can make the skid worse.

  2. Ease off the gas

    Let the car slow down naturally.

  3. Steer straight

    Hold the wheel steady in the direction you want to go.

  4. Wait for grip

    Once you feel the tires bite again, continue slowly.

Pre-rain car check

  • Wiper blades: no streaks or squeaks; washer fluid filled
  • Tire tread and pressure: worn tires hydroplane sooner. See my tire care guide
  • All lights working, including brake lights
  • Brakes checked at PMS
  • Payong and a towel in the car

When the rain turns into flooding, follow my flood rules and after-flood checklist, and keep an emergency kit in the trunk.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use hazard lights while driving in heavy rain?

Use your headlights while moving. Hazards are for when you're stopped or a hazard to others; using them while moving can confuse drivers about your signals.

What do I do if my car hydroplanes?

Don't brake hard or jerk the wheel. Ease off the accelerator, hold the steering straight, and let the tires regain grip.

How often should I replace wiper blades?

When they streak, skip or squeak. Many owners check them before the rainy season; heat can also harden the rubber.

How much following distance in rain?

At least double your dry-weather gap. A 3-second gap in dry weather becomes 6 seconds or more in heavy rain.
