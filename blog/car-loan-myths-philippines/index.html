Car Loan Philippines: 6 Myths, Debunked

By Richard Cuagdan, Mitsubishi Sales Executive · 2026-10-21

Quick answer: Getting a car loan in the Philippines is easier to plan once the myths are cleared. Most car loan myths in the Philippines are half-truths. Banks don't only look at salary size; they check your capacity to pay, your documents and your credit history. Self-employed buyers and OFWs can apply with the right documents, and cash isn't automatically cheaper once you compare promos and what your money could do elsewhere.

"Hindi ka maaaprubahan, maliit sahod mo." "Bawal ang self-employed." "OFW ka, mahirap 'yan." I hear these sabi-sabi every week on the showroom floor. Some are outdated, some are half-true, and some keep good buyers from even trying. Let's go through them.

Myth 1: You need a big salary to get approved

Fact: Banks look at your capacity to pay, meaning how your income compares with the monthly and your other obligations, not just the size of your salary. A modest earner with few debts and complete documents can be a stronger applicant than a high earner with many loans. The unit and terms you choose matter too, which is why I always start with the budget calculator.

Myth 2: Self-employed buyers can't get a car loan

Fact: Business owners apply all the time. You just prove income differently. Banks commonly ask for:

  • DTI or SEC registration, or a mayor's/business permit
  • Latest ITR and financial statements
  • Business bank statements for the past several months
  • For some banks, trade references from suppliers or clients

Myth 3: OFWs abroad can't apply

Fact: Many banks accept OFW applicants. Common requirements include your passport, employment or crew contract, recent payslips and proof of remittance. If you're abroad, a special power of attorney (SPA) or a co-borrower in the Philippines may be required. More in my OFW car buying guide.

Myth 4: Cash is always cheaper

Fact: Paying cash means no interest, but it's not automatically the better deal. Promos and inclusions can differ between cash and financing, and draining your savings leaves you without an emergency fund or business capital. Compare the total cost and what your cash could do elsewhere before deciding.

Myth 5: One rejection means you can never get a car loan

Fact: A declined application usually has a reason: incomplete documents, high existing debt or a unit beyond your capacity. Fix the cause, choose a unit that fits, or add a co-borrower, then apply again.

Myth 6: The monthly is the only thing you need to think about

Fact: Banks check if you can afford the monthly; you should check if you can afford the car. Add fuel, insurance renewal, PMS, parking and tolls. My first-time car buyer checklist covers the full list.

What banks actually check

  1. Capacity

    Income versus the monthly and your existing obligations.

  2. Documents

    Complete, consistent and verifiable IDs, income proof and proof of billing.

  3. Credit history

    How you've handled past loans and credit cards.

  4. Stability

    Length of employment or years in business.

How to prepare before applying

  • Gather your documents early. Here's the car loan requirements list.
  • Settle or reduce small debts if you can.
  • Pick a unit that fits your budget, not just your dream.
  • Be honest and consistent in your application.
  • Ask your sales executive to pre-check your documents before submission.

Whether you're eyeing the Xpander for the family or the Destinator, the process is the same: prepare, apply, and let the bank evaluate. Approval is always subject to bank evaluation.

Frequently asked questions

Can a self-employed person get a car loan in the Philippines?

Yes. Banks typically ask for business registration (DTI, SEC or mayor's permit), ITR or financial statements, and bank statements. Requirements vary by bank.

Can an OFW get a car loan while abroad?

Yes. Banks commonly ask for the employment or crew contract, payslips and proof of remittance, and may require a special power of attorney (SPA) or a co-borrower in the Philippines.

What do banks check for car loan approval?

Your capacity to pay (income versus obligations), complete and verifiable documents, and your credit history. Approval is always subject to bank evaluation.

Is it better to pay cash for a car?

Not always. Compare the cash price with financing promos and inclusions, and consider whether keeping your savings as an emergency fund or business capital is worth more to you.
